# Task 2: Phishing Email Detection and Awareness

## Objective
To study sample emails, identify phishing indicators, classify email risk, and provide security awareness recommendations.

## Sample Email 1: Class Timetable Update

**Subject:** Class timetable update

**Email content:**
Hi students, the updated class timetable is available on the college portal. Please check it during your next login.

**Classification:** Likely safe — based on the provided text only.

**Reason:** The message gives a routine update and does not ask for passwords or verification codes. The sender and any links have not been verified.

## Sample Email 2: Account Needs Attention

**Subject:** Your account needs attention

**Email content:**
Your account may be restricted. Please review your account activity as soon as possible.

**Classification:** Suspicious — further verification required.

**Reason:** The message uses a warning about account restrictions but does not provide enough context to verify its legitimacy. Check the sender independently and avoid clicking unexpected links.

## Sample Email 3: Urgent Account Closure

**Subject:** Urgent — account will be closed

**Email content:**
Your account will be closed in 10 minutes. Reply with your password and verification code to prevent closure.

**Classification:** Phishing indicators present — treat as phishing.

**Reason:** The message uses extreme urgency and asks for a password and verification code. Never share credentials or verification codes through email.

## Common Phishing Warning Signs

- Urgent threats or pressure to act immediately.
- Requests for passwords, verification codes, or sensitive information.
- Unexpected links or attachments.
- Sender addresses that do not match the claimed organization.
- Spelling, grammar, or branding inconsistencies.

## Safety Recommendations

1. Do not reply with passwords or verification codes.
2. Do not open unexpected links or attachments.
3. Verify requests through the organization's official website or known contact details.
4. Report suspected phishing to the relevant IT or security team.
5. Enable multi-factor authentication where available.

## Limitations

These are fictional training examples, not real collected emails. Classification is based only on the text provided. Sender identity, email headers, links, and attachments were not analyzed, so the examples do not demonstrate a fully automated email detection system.
